\chapter{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

\section{Introduction}

This chapter presents the experimental evaluation of the routing mechanism
described in Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}. The evaluation was conducted
on physical hardware in a three-node scenario representative of the target
application. The following sections describe the hardware platform, the test
methodology, and the results obtained.


\section{Hardware Platform and Test Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The test setup comprised three nodes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Node 1 (N1) --- Robot}: AlphaBot2-Pi mobile robot equipped
  with a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (4\,GB RAM), a Raspberry Pi Camera Module v2
  for video capture, and a USB Wi-Fi adapter operating in ad-hoc (IBSS) mode.
  The robot runs the RA-TDMAs+ node software and the \texttt{alphabot\_node.py}
  teleoperation server.

  \item \textbf{Node 2 (N2) --- Relay}: A Raspberry Pi 4 Model B with a
  USB Wi-Fi adapter, operating as a stationary relay node running only the
  RA-TDMAs+ node software.

  \item \textbf{Node 3 (N3) --- Base Station}: A laptop computer running
  Ubuntu, connected via Wi-Fi ad-hoc and running the RA-TDMAs+ node software
  and the \texttt{base\_station.py} operator interface with video display and
  joystick control.
\end{itemize}

All nodes were configured in Wi-Fi ad-hoc mode (IBSS) on the same SSID and
channel, with physical IP addresses in the \texttt{172.20.10.0/24} subnet and
mesh IP addresses in the \texttt{10.0.0.0/24} subnet via the TUN interface.

The video stream was generated by GStreamer on N1 at 640$\times$480 pixels,
15\,fps, with a target bitrate of approximately 500\,kbps. Network traffic
was captured using \texttt{tcpdump -i any} on N3 for post-experiment analysis.

\subsection{Topology Control}

Link failures between N1 and N3 were induced by physically obstructing the
radio path (placing the nodes in separate rooms or behind metallic obstacles),
causing the RSSI to drop below the association threshold and the link to break
within one TDMA frame.


\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

The following metrics were used to characterise system performance:

\begin{description}
  \item[Convergence time] Time elapsed from the last video packet received
  via the direct link to the first video packet received via the relay path.
  Measured from PCAP timestamps.
  \item[Round-trip latency (RTT)] Round-trip time for ICMP echo requests
  from N3 to N1 during steady-state operation, measured with \texttt{ping}.
  \item[Inter-arrival time (IA)] Time between consecutive video UDP packets
  arriving at N3, measured from PCAP timestamps. Reported separately for
  the direct-link phase (before failure) and the relay phase (after convergence).
  \item[Throughput] Average UDP throughput of the video stream at N3,
  measured in kbps.
  \item[Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR)] Fraction of transmitted MSG\_DATA
  packets received, estimated from sequence number gaps in the TDMA header.
\end{description}


\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Routing Convergence}

Six link-failure experiments were conducted. In four of the six runs (67\%),
the system successfully converged to relay routing and the video stream was
restored. Table~\ref{tab:convergence} summarises the convergence times for
the successful runs.

The mean convergence time of approximately 2.76\,s was dominated by the
\texttt{VIDEO\_LOSS\_TIMEOUT} of 1.5\,s at the base station, which must
expire before N3 sends the feedback signal that triggers relay activation at
N1. The remaining ~1.26\,s comprise the feedback propagation delay (one TDMA
slot), the routing recomputation (measured in microseconds), the Netlink route
installation, and the time for the first relay packet to traverse N2 and
arrive at N3.

The mean direct RTT of approximately 48\,ms is consistent with an expected
wait of up to one TDMA frame (150\,ms) averaged over the slot position
distribution, considering that the ICMP echo can be sent at any point within
the frame. The relay RTT of approximately 97\,ms reflects the additional
traversal of one extra TDMA slot (approximately 50\,ms per direction), as
expected from the two-hop path.

\subsection{Video Stream Inter-Arrival}

The video stream produced by GStreamer arrives at N3 in bursts aligned with
the TDMA slot structure: N1 accumulates packets during the 100\,ms it is
not transmitting, then sends a burst of approximately 6--9 packets during its
50\,ms slot. The inter-arrival distribution is therefore bimodal:
sub-millisecond gaps within a burst, and approximately 150\,ms gaps between
bursts.

After relay activation, the burst timing shifts by approximately 50\,ms (one
TDMA slot), but the inter-burst gap remains at 150\,ms. This demonstrates
that the relay is transparent to the delivery rhythm of the video stream:
the burst structure is preserved and no additional jitter is introduced at
the frame level.

\subsection{Throughput}

The measured throughput of the video stream at N3 was approximately
580--610\,kbps, slightly above the 500\,kbps target bitrate. The excess
arises from two sources: MPEG-TS packetization overhead (approximately 15\%)
and the use of \texttt{-g 1} in the encoder configuration, which disables
inter-frame prediction and produces all-intra frames with no temporal
compression gain.

Under relay conditions, no measurable throughput degradation was observed
compared to direct-link conditions, confirming that the relay slot (50\,ms)
provides sufficient bandwidth for the video stream.

\subsection{Discussion}

The experimental results confirm that the proposed routing mechanism achieves
its primary objectives: reliable relay activation after link failure and
transparent delivery of IP traffic across a two-hop path. The dominant
limitation is the convergence time, which is dominated by the video loss
detection timeout (1.5\,s) rather than by routing computation or route
installation overhead. Reducing \texttt{VIDEO\_LOSS\_TIMEOUT} to 0.5\,s
would cut the expected convergence time to approximately 1.26\,s, at the
cost of increased sensitivity to transient packet losses.

The two failed convergence runs (out of six) were attributed to the relay
route not being fully established before the video stream timeout expired at
the base station, causing the base station to stop sending feedback before
N1 had processed the first feedback signal. This race condition can be
mitigated by repeating the feedback signal for a longer period, which
\texttt{base\_station.py} already implements via the
\texttt{FEEDBACK\_INTERVAL} parameter.
